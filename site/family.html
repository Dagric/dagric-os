One house. Every computer.

The proposed Family Service would provide hosted delivery, guided upgrades, and support for a defined number of concurrently managed computers in one household. It would not sell or limit a licence to Dagric OS or any included component.

Parental controls are not what this sells. Screen time and bedtime limits are free on every edition of Dagric OS, including the free one, and always will be — they are not part of this service, not part of Pro, and not something we will ever put behind a price. If parental controls are what brought you here, you do not need the Family Service and you do not need Pro. The honest state of that work is further down this page.

This household service is not currently offered. Its price, machine capacity, checkout terms, and delivery workflow have not been finalized or verified. The release-status page is the source of truth for current availability.

Several computers means several chances for something not to work. Find out before you spend anything: the free edition is the same system underneath, one USB stick will boot every machine in the house without touching a disk, and every stick carries Check This PC — a plain-language report on that machine's Wi-Fi, graphics, sound, Secure Boot and drives. Run it on each computer first. And trying free lets you evaluate the same operating-system foundation. Any future Family Service delivery or guided-upgrade workflow will be described only after it is implemented and tested.

What a household actually gets

Each supported household computer would receive the same Pro build. The paid item would be the hosted delivery, guided-upgrade, and support service around that build.

The creative suite, on each machine

GIMP with PhotoGIMP's Photoshop-style layout, Krita, Inkscape, Kdenlive, Blender, OBS Studio, darktable for RAW photos, Audacity and HandBrake — preinstalled and working on first boot on each computer managed through the service.

Windows software

Wine 10.0 and winetricks preinstalled, including 32-bit Wine for classic .exe installers. The free edition ships no Wine at all, so this is a real difference on each machine — the household's one stubborn Windows program has somewhere to run whichever computer it lands on.

The gaming runtime

The Mesa Vulkan drivers in both 64- and 32-bit, where the free edition ships the Vulkan loader with no Mesa driver behind it, plus GameMode, MangoHud with goverlay, and Lutris. The launchers themselves — Steam, Heroic, Bottles — are one click on the free edition too; what Pro puts underneath them is the part that shows up as frames.

Windows in a window

A full KVM virtual-machine stack with UEFI and TPM 2.0, so even Windows 11 installs inside Dagric. Genuinely Pro-only: the hypervisor, virt-manager and the TPM emulator are not on the free edition. You bring the Windows ISO; we never bundle it.

The developer stack

Docker and Compose, Podman, Distrobox, build-essential, Git, Python and SSH on whichever machine in the house needs them. Podman runs rootless immediately; the Docker daemon and the SSH server ship switched off until someone turns them on — one command each — so nothing in the house listens uninvited.

The security suite

The opt-in OpenSnitch application firewall, and USB Protection that allow-lists your own devices before it blocks anything. Armed when you choose, on each machine, never at the cost of somebody's keyboard.

Full office and CJK fonts

The complete LibreOffice suite, where the free edition ships Writer, Calc, Impress and Draw — plus Noto CJK — the 89 MB set that adds proper Japanese and Chinese typographic forms and Serif CJK. The free edition already renders Chinese, Japanese and Korean without boxes; this is the typography on top. Ordinary accented and symbol text renders correctly on both editions.

All seven Looks and Styles

Seven desktop layouts against the free edition's three, and seven colour Styles against four — so the computer in the study and the computer in the kitchen do not have to look like the same computer.

Scheduled restore points

Timeshift restore points that you schedule, on top of the automatic snapshot-before-every-update that both editions already take. Worth more across several machines than one: it is the difference between fixing a household's computer and rebuilding it.

The privacy toolkit

KeePassXC for a local password vault only you hold, Syncthing to sync files directly between the household's own machines with no cloud and no account, Flatseal to see what each app is allowed to touch, qBittorrent and Filelight.

One household service. Clearly defined support scope.

The service would cover hosted delivery, guided upgrades, curation, integration, testing, and support for its stated number of concurrently managed household computers. Every included component remains under its own licence. The service does not reduce anyone's right to run, copy, study, modify, or redistribute a component under that licence.

Parental controls are free. Always.

Not part of this service. Not part of Pro. Free on every edition, forever, and that is a decision rather than an introductory offer.

Brazil legislated screen-time and bedtime controls, and Brazilian Portuguese is one of the six languages Dagric OS ships in. Putting legal compliance behind a paid service in the country that wrote the law is not compliance, so screen time and bedtime limits are free on the free edition, free on Pro, and free with any future Family Service. There is no version of Dagric OS in which a parent has to pay to limit a child's computer, and there will not be one.

Being honest about where that stands: Family Limits ships today — daily screen time, a bedtime the computer keeps for you, and a choice between locking the screen and signing out when the time is up. It is free, on every edition, and you will not be asked for anything to get it. If parental controls are the only reason you are on this page, you do not need to buy anything. Download the free edition, or write to us and we will tell you when they land. A future Family Service would not provide parental controls sooner, and we would rather say so than suggest otherwise.

How the service would work

The commercial scope is a household delivery, guided-upgrade, and support service. The software itself keeps the rights granted by every applicable component licence.

Defined household capacity

The published offer would state exactly how many concurrently managed computers receive hosted delivery, guided upgrades, and support.

Component rights stay intact

Nothing in the household service would replace or narrow the GPL, CC BY-SA, or any other applicable component licence. Those licences control how the software and artwork may be used, copied, modified, and redistributed.

Service scope, not software control

The machine count would meter only the paid service. It would not make continued local use of the installed operating system depend on a paid licence or recurring authorization.

Mechanics must be verified first

This draft makes no claim about an entitlement-verification or transfer workflow. Those details will be published only after the household implementation, privacy review, and support procedure are tested.

"One household" would define the service scope: computers in one home owned or managed by the people who live there. It would not restrict what recipients may lawfully do with open-source components, and it would not be represented as an office, school, club, or reseller support plan.

The Family Service may not fit if…

Reasons this proposed service may not fit, even after it is ready.

…you came here for the parental controls

They are free on every edition and they are not part of this service. Paying for this would add nothing to them. The full state of that is above — and the honest answer for a parent with one computer is the free edition, at no cost.

…you need support for only one computer

Use the current Pro service description instead. The household service is intended only for people who want delivery, guided upgrades, and support across several computers.

…those computers are nearly full

Pro needs 40 GB free on each machine against the free edition's 25 GB, because the creative suite, the Wine and gaming runtimes and the CJK fonts are really on the disk. Across a house of older laptops that is a constraint that repeats, and it will not get better after you have paid. Check every machine before considering a future household service.

…you only wanted Steam, Bottles, Heroic or Ollama

All four are one click from the Hub on the free edition, on every computer in the house, for nothing — and so are ProtonUp-Qt, ONLYOFFICE, Joplin, LocalSend, Cryptomator and Upscayl. If that list is the whole reason, install free everywhere. Pro's gaming value is the 32-bit Vulkan, GameMode and MangoHud underneath them.

…it is not really one household

This proposed support service is for computers in one home. It is not an office, school, club, or reseller support plan. If you need an organization-wide service, ask us rather than relying on this draft.
