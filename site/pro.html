Windows apps & serious gaming

Straight answer, same as further down the page: three of these four are one click from the Hub on the free edition too — we don't gate a download button. What Pro buys is the runtime underneath each one, which is the part that takes assembling. Each card says exactly what that is.

Bottles + Wine Also free

Run Windows software in separate per-app Wine environments tuned for compatibility — not security sandboxes — with an install helper on either edition. The Pro half is underneath it: Wine 10.0 and winetricks preinstalled, plus 32-bit Wine for classic .exe installers. The free edition ships no Wine at all.

Steam + Proton Also free

The consent install is one click on both editions. What Pro has waiting for it is the stack Proton actually runs on: the Mesa Vulkan drivers in both 64- and 32-bit, where the free edition ships the Vulkan loader with no Mesa driver behind it — plus GameMode and MangoHud with the goverlay configurator, all preinstalled and tuned. That is the genuine difference, and it is the one that shows up as frames.

Heroic & ProtonUp-Qt Also free

Your Epic, GOG, and Amazon libraries — plus point-and-click GE-Proton management. One click from the Hub on either edition, like Steam and Bottles beside it. Pro preinstalls Lutris alongside them for the Battle.net, EA and Ubisoft installers.

Windows in a window Pro only

For the rare app Wine can't run: a full KVM virtual-machine stack — UEFI and TPM 2.0 included, so even Windows 11 installs. Real Windows, safely boxed inside Dagric. You bring the Windows ISO; we never bundle it. This one genuinely is Pro: the hypervisor, virt-manager and the TPM emulator are not on the free edition, which is why the Hub hides the row there rather than offering a button that can't work.

Don't buy Pro if…

Four reasons to keep your $39. We would rather lose the sale than take it from somebody who was going to want it back.

…you only wanted Steam, Heroic, Bottles or Ollama

All four are one click from the Hub on the free edition, and so are ProtonUp-Qt, GE-Proton, ONLYOFFICE, Joplin, LocalSend, Cryptomator and Upscayl. If that list is your whole reason for buying, download the free ISO instead — you will get the same buttons. Pro's gaming value is the 32-bit Vulkan, GameMode and MangoHud underneath them, which matters if you play, and not otherwise.

…the disk is nearly full

Pro needs 40 GB of free space against the free edition's 25 GB, because the creative suite, the Wine and gaming runtimes and the CJK fonts are all really there on the disk. On a small SSD or an older laptop that is a real constraint, and it will not get better after you have paid.

…you are comfortable with a package manager

Every application Pro preinstalls — GIMP, Krita, Inkscape, Blender, Kdenlive, OBS, darktable, Audacity, KeePassXC and the rest — is free software, from Debian's own archive, under its own licence. You can install every one of them on the free edition yourself, for nothing, and we would not think less of you. What the $39 buys is that somebody already did it, wired the pieces together, and tested that the result works on first boot.

…you need DaVinci Resolve preinstalled

Dagric has not obtained permission to redistribute Resolve, so the Hub walks you through Blackmagic's own download on both editions equally. Buying Pro does not get Resolve installed for you. Kdenlive is preinstalled and is a real video editor; if it isn't the one you need, that is worth knowing before checkout rather than after.

Still here? Then the things above don't apply to you, and the rest of this page is what you get.
