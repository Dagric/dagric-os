LibreOffice Impress

Slide decks, and presenting them without a fight.

On Windows this wasMicrosoft PowerPoint

What it is

Impress makes slide decks and opens .pptx files. Outline view, speaker notes, slide masters, transitions and animations are all present under names PowerPoint users will recognise.

Five things you will actually do

  1. Start a deck from an outline

    View → Outline, then type your headings and press Tab to indent bullet points. Switch back to Normal view and you have a full deck. This is much faster than dragging text boxes around.

  2. Present on a projector

    Plug in, then press F5. If the projector shows your notes instead of your slides, swap the primary display in System Settings → Display & Monitor, or use Slide Show → Slide Show Settings to choose which screen gets the presentation.

  3. Use presenter notes

    View → Notes to write them. With two screens connected, the Presenter Console shows your notes, a timer and the next slide on your laptop while the audience sees only the slide.

  4. Change the look of every slide at once

    View → Master Slide. Edit the master and the change lands on every slide that uses it, which is how you fix a font choice you regret on slide forty.

  5. Hand out a PDF

    File → Export as PDF. The dialogue can export handouts with several slides to a page, and can include your notes or leave them out.

One thing that will surprise you

Fonts are the thing that breaks a borrowed deck, not features. A .pptx built around a font this machine does not have will re-lay-out and your careful text boxes will overflow. Dagric ships the Calibri- and Cambria-metric substitutes, which covers most corporate decks. For anything else, the fix is to open the deck, note the missing font, and either install it or pick a replacement once in the slide master.

Where to find it

Launcher → LibreOffice Impress, or search PowerPoint or presentation.
