Webcam photos and video

Use guvcview to check your camera and save a local photo or recording.

On Windows this wasthe Camera app

What it is

guvcview previews a supported webcam and captures photos or video. Its preview and controls can appear in separate windows. It is a camera recorder, so use OBS Studio when you want to record the desktop instead.

Five things you will actually do

  1. Check the preview

    Open the camera's privacy shutter, connect it if needed, and launch guvcview. If you have more than one camera, choose the intended device in its video settings.

  2. Set a useful picture

    Choose a resolution and frame rate supported by the camera. Start with automatic exposure and white balance. Lower the resolution or frame rate if the preview stutters.

  3. Take a photo

    Check the output filename and folder, then use the photo or image-capture control. Open the saved file in Dolphin to confirm the result.

  4. Record a short test

    Choose the video destination and check audio settings if you need a microphone. Start recording, speak and move for a few seconds, then stop. Play the saved clip to check both picture and sound before recording something important.

  5. Finish cleanly

    Stop recording before closing the app so the file can finish saving. Close the preview and controls, then close the camera shutter if it has one.

If the camera is missing or busy

Close video calls, browser camera tabs, and other recording apps, then try again. Check the privacy switch, USB connection, and device selection. Camera controls depend on the hardware, so an unavailable setting does not always mean the app is broken. Use Check This PC or Support if no camera appears.

Where your recordings go

The filename and destination are selected in guvcview's capture settings. Check them before recording; do not assume the file went into Videos. Record only people who have agreed to be recorded, and review your surroundings before sharing.
