Writing and documents

Okular

Read, annotate, fill in and sign PDFs — and about a dozen other formats.

On Windows this wasAdobe Acrobat Reader, and Edge's PDF viewer

What it is

Okular is the document viewer: PDF above all, but also ePub, DjVu, comic-book archives, TIFF and PostScript. It is a reader with real teeth — highlighting, notes, form filling, digital signatures and a presentation mode — without any of the upsell.

Five things you will actually do

  1. Highlight and annotate

    F6 opens the annotation toolbar. Highlight, underline, freehand, sticky notes and stamps. Your marks are saved into the PDF when you save it, so the other end sees them in any reader.

  2. Fill in a form

    If the PDF has real form fields, Okular shows a bar offering to show them; click into the boxes and type. Save, and email it back. For a form that is just a scan, use the annotation tools to type on top of it instead.

  3. Sign a document

    Tools → Digitally Sign… for a real cryptographic signature if you have a certificate. For an ordinary “sign here”, use the freehand annotation tool or a stamp of a scanned signature — that is what most people mean and it is accepted.

  4. Print only some of the pages

    Ctrl+P, then a page range like 1,4-8. The print dialogue also does two-sided, several pages to a sheet, and booklet order.

  5. Present from a PDF

    Ctrl+Shift+P enters presentation mode: one page at a time, full screen, with a drawing pen available. Handy when you have a PDF of a deck and no deck.

One thing that will surprise you

Okular opens read-only and it looks like a bug. By default Okular is in browse mode and clicking on text does nothing much, which reads as “this program cannot do anything”. Press F6 and the annotation tools appear; the whole second half of the application is behind that one key.

Where to find it

Launcher → Document Viewer (also found by typing Okular, PDF or Acrobat). Any PDF you double-click opens here.
